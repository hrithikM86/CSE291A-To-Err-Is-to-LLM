Algorithm for `largest_prime_factor(n: int)`:

1. **Initialization**:
   - Set `largest_factor` to 2, the smallest prime number.

2. **Factorization Loop**:
   - While `n` is even, divide `n` by 2 and update `largest_factor` to 2.
   - Set `factor` to 3.
   - While `factor * factor` is less than or equal to `n`:
     - If `n` is divisible by `factor`, divide `n` by `factor` and update `largest_factor` to `factor`.
     - Otherwise, increment `factor` by 2 (to skip even numbers).

3. **Final Check**:
   - If `n` is greater than 1 after the loop, `n` itself is a prime factor and is the largest.

4. **Return**:
   - Return `largest_factor`.

**Edge Cases**:
- If `n` is a power of a prime (e.g., 2048), the loop will eventually reduce `n` to 2.
- If `n` is a product of multiple primes (e.g., 13195), the loop will find and update the largest prime factor.
- For large `n`, the algorithm efficiently reduces `n` by its smallest prime factors first, ensuring performance.

**Complexity**:
- Time complexity: O(√n) due to the square root limit in the while loop.
- Space complexity: O(1) as no additional space is used.
